## Everyone Has Issues
For the final project of ICS 314, me an my two teammates implemented Agile Project Management (APM)&mdash;an approach that breaks large projects into smaller tasks. More precisely, we used a style of APM called Issue Driven Project Management (IDPM). The main rundown is that each team member is assigned at least one issue (fittingly named), which are all neatly organized in some sort of chart for all members to see clearly. Each member works on their "issue" separately, and then after a week or so, all members reconvene, deliver their work, and combines it with the others. For my team, we called those times "milestones".

I think there is a lot of value in this type of project management, especially beyond the realm of software engineering. Whether you are making a powerpoint for a class presentation, planning a huge event, etc., the general idea of breaking everything up into "issues" and setting clear expectations and deadlines can be useful for almost any collaborative project.

It can be a huge motivator to others, knowing that they have teammates relying on them to get their work done on time
For one, it sets the expectations for each person very clearly, and shows to those who do their work, that they are meeting their teammates' expectations and making meaingful contributions.
