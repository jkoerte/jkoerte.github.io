## Everybody's Got Issues
For the final project of ICS 314, me and my two teammates learned to implement Agile Project Management (APM)&mdash;an approach that breaks large projects into smaller tasks. More precisely, we used a style of APM called Issue Driven Project Management (IDPM). The main rundown is that each team member is assigned at least one "issue" (fittingly named), which are all neatly organized in some sort of chart for all members to see clearly. Each member works on their "issue" separately, and then on some agreed upon day after about a week or so, all members reconvene, deliver their work, and combines it with the others. For my team, we called these days "milestones".

I think there is a lot of value in this type of project management, especially beyond the realm of web applications. Whether you are making a powerpoint for a class presentation, planning a huge event, etc., the general idea of breaking everything up into "issues" and setting clear expectations and deadlines can be useful for almost any collaborative project.

Breaking up the work makes the task much less daunting than if you were to look at the task as a whole, giving each teammate more flexibility to work and creates a less stressful environment. Setting clear expectations gives each teammate a direction to work towards, and it can be a huge motivator to some, knowing that they have teammates relying on them to get their work done on time. And finally, creating these "issues" in a place for all to access and discuss allows for realistic assignment based on a teammate's skillset and/or availability, etc.

So, maybe for your next team project, you should get some "issues" too...
