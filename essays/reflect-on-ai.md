## I. Introduction
AI in *anything* is undoubtedly a very controversial topic at the moment, and as for its role in education, English teachers might have some strong words on the matter...

The current president of UH&mdash;Wendy Hensel, on the other hand, seems quite enthusiastic about implementing it.

Here we have two sides of the argument, but my views fall somewhere in-between. I believe AI is a great tool to aid students' education, but not replace it. I am of the belief that students, including myself, still need to learn the topics and try to understand the information before chucking a prompt into an AI; and when students do use AI for assistance, they should also strive to understand the generated response and question its accuracy. In this way, students are not relying on AI for answers, but using it to build on what they already know.

I am also of the belief that for things less focused on data and numbers&mdash;things that requires someone's own ideas and rhetoric, AI cannot replicate them.

In terms of software engineering, there is a lot of information to process, such a syntax, databases, ORMs, and so on. Therefore, having AI to assist you and fill gaps in your knowledge is a very helpful tool, but still, you should double-check and test everything yourself, and at least understand the basics beforehand, whether that be through documentation or something else.

### II. Personal Experience With AI
#### 1. Experience quizzes e.g. E18
I personally wanted to attempt all the practice WODs without the use of AI to better understand the concepts in the WOD. Also because there was always a video for me to watch showing the process of the programming done by one of the UH ICS professors, I could use that instead after attempting it on my own to better understand and learn.

#### 2. Practice quizzes
Similar to the Experience quizzes I wanted to attempt all of them without the use of AI to better understand the concepts and prepare me for the graded quizzes

#### 3. Quizzes
Towards the beginning of the semester I felt that I should not use AI on the quizzes, but as the semester progressed, I realized I was only hurting myself by not using all the tools available to me. I used it to remind myself of concepts such as all the Array methods that I was a bit foggy on, to check snippets of code where I suspected errors were coming from, and to check if ideas I suggested might work for the quiz requirements. Typically I would ask something like: "Would `<function/code here>` work for this part of the `<assignments requirement here>`?"

#### 4. Essays
I did not use AI to write any of my technical essays this semester, mostly because I have written many essays for my other classes such as HWST and AMST, and it has been drilled into my head not to use AI for said assignments. On top of that, even the ICS 314 website does not recommend using AI for writing essays

#### 5. Final project
I used AI a lot for my final project, especially because I had very limited experience with PostgreSQL, Prisma, and React/Nextjs. Since we were given a website template, I often used AI to explain certain things in there such as the Prisma database query methods as well as help with certain React Bootstrap components not working as intended. Among these, I asked for help understanding various other things such as how the PostgreSQL database works with `schema.prsima` and what values are given and initially hidden when you call `findUnique()` or `findMany()` One prompt I gave Google Gemini was: "Why does `session.user` not include my added fields in the User model in `schema.prisma`"?

The responses given by Gemini were very useful, and did not take much time to get a workable result.

#### 6. Learning a concept / tutorial
When learning a concept/tutorial, I would often try it on my own first, and whatever I did not understand, I would ask AI for clarification based on what I did understand, as well as follow-up questions to get into the specifics of what I wanted to know.

The responses given by Gemini were very useful, although they may take a while to understand, but that comes with the process of learning things.

#### 7. Answering a question in class or in Discord
I have never used AI to answer a question in class or in Discord, mainly because I feel that even with AI, I am not knowledgeable enough to answer any questions.

#### 8. Asking or answering a smart-question
I have never used AI to answer a smart-question for the same reason as above.

I have also never used AI to ask a smart-question, because there was a whole guideline about asking smart-questions that I had read and an assignment to write an essay about said topic, so I just tried my best to follow it by showing I did some of my own research already and trying to be as clear as possible. 

#### 9. Coding example e.g. “give an example of using Underscore .pluck”
As much as I do ask AI for help understanding concepts and code blocks, I have never really asked it to give me coding examples. Mostly I will just give prompts similar to the ones I mentioned above like: "How does `<code here>` work?" and then try and figure it out using other examples, especially since I have several ICS templates and my finished WOD assignments to look back at for examples.

#### 10. Explaining code
I often ask prompts like "Explain how `<code here>` works" such as when I was struggling with database concepts for my final project, so I would often look at the code already there, or other components being imported and get an explanation on what it does.

The responses given by Gemini were very useful, and did not take much time to get a workable result.

#### 11. Writing code
For React Bootstrap, because there are so many classes and props for each component, I would often ask Gemini something like: "How do I get my `<react component here>` to `<desired specifications here>`". Mostly I had trouble with centering my components and their contents using things like `justify-content-center`

Often, the response from Gemini was not able to solve my problem, and so I had to figure it out on my own.

In terms of more advanced things like Prisma functions, I would usually ask something like: "Why does `<code here>` not work?" and Gemini would usually write me out why it was broken on top of a code solution.

The response from Gemini was very useful on top of the explanation as to how the code would fix my problem. It also did not take much time to get a workable result in my experience

#### 12. Documenting code
I actually have not used AI for code documentation. It is a bad practice of mine that I need to improve on, but I often do not leave comments throughout my code. The only other time I had to do documentation was a mockup of our milestone 1 home page for for the final project. I did not need to use AI here either, because there was already a template made by the ICS professors which I used as a guide instead.

#### 13. Quality assurance e.g. "What's wrong with this code `<code here>`" or "Fix the ESLint errors in `<code here>`"
This is probably what I use AI the most for in my time programming. I run into a lot of problems while coding, so it is very convenient for me to just ask: "Why does `<code here>` not work?", and then have a way to immediately figure out exactly what is wrong, or get a general idea otherwise. Oftentimes it is usually just a small error that takes a few seconds to fix, that I might have spent several minutes trying to find. However, I do not just copy-and-past whatever code Gemini gives me, I read exactly what it tells me on why it was incorrect.

The responses given by Gemini were very useful in helping me to identify the problem, and did not take much time to verify and get a workable result.

#### 14. Other uses in ICS 314 not listed
Sometimes if a WOD really confused me, I would ask Gemini to break it down and explain in more simple words what the instructions were asking for, such as: "Can you explain what `<instructions here>` is exactly asking for?"

## III. Impact on Learning and Understanding
I believe AI has positively impacted my learning experience so far. I feel that it provided good explanations and solutions to my problems. 

In regards to comprehension, I feel that the explanations provided by AI are quite good, and you are free to ask as many follow-up questions as needed until you can fully comprehend a concept. 

When working on the backend parts of our final project I found that after asking Gemini, the less and less I would need to do so again when working on similar things. In that regard, I feel like you can still develop your software engineering skills even with AI so long as you do not just copy-and-paste the code and move on. 

However, despite the benefits, I do not think my problem-solving skills have received much at all. Whenever I run into a problem, instead of trying to figure it out on my own, I just jump straight to Gemini for help. While AI is certainly useful, it is a good skill to be able to identify and solve a problem based only on errors or output.

Overall, I had no experience with software engineering before taking this class, so AI has only helped to improve my understanding of software engineering concepts

## IV. Practical Applications
An interesting practical application I can think of in the real world is the use of Gen-AI in game development. There is some controversy around it use, but many studios seem to use it for generating placeholders, and it is generally agreed upon that using it to clear out the menial tasks allows developers to spend more time on the challenging parts, saving both development time and resources. I am sure the same concept can be used to address real-world software engineering problems, such as making more time for software engineers to work on harder tasks when on tight deadlines from clients. However, my only frame of reference for projects is small in comparison to the large systems many companies have, and I feel that AI might struggle to be nearly as useful as it was for my project.

## V. Challenges and Opportunities
I think the main challenge I faced when using AI was that it is not always accurate, but that is to be expected. I remember specifically all the times it would recommend me to use `justify-content-center` or `align-items-center` and nothing would happen. Also, it would very rarely generate answers that were too complicated for me, so I would not use them, and instead inquire in a different way to get an answer that more so matched my knowledge level.

In regards to opportunities for further integration of AI in software engineering education, the first thing I would think of is having AI to explain challenging concepts to students. I have also seen people use AI to create schedules for them, and perhaps that could be of benefit to implement in the ICS course websites somehow?

## VI. Comparative Analysis
Compared to the classic textbook and lectures I found it is much more engaging to...*engage* in a conversation with AI, where it feels like you are making meaningful progress towards understanding a concept or fixing a problem with each question you ask.

But, the most engaging thing of all, is sitting down and getting to work on a problem/project yourself. Perhaps AI-enhanced learning might have the means to generate exercises tailored to a students current level to boost their confidence and skills in programming, rather than frustrate them out of it. 

I also believe that a curriculum tailored to the students' needs will aid in the development of practical software engineering skills.

This also ties into the idea of knowledge retention, since one way to increase the chance of knowledge retention is through engaging the students in the content. It could be argued that AI actually reduces students' need to retain knowledge, but as long as they are taking it seriously and trying to understand the explanations that the AI gives, I think it could be just as good as reading a textbook or lecture, and perhaps better, even. In the end, it is up to the student; they retain as much knowledge as they care to pay attention to, regardless of whether its from a book or a bot.

## VII. Future Considerations
I am hopeful for the future that AI will significantly enhance and streamline software engineering education. A potential advancement may be the ability to have all software engineering classes be async, as content would be specifically tuned to an individual student. Perhaps some challenges may be professors needing to regulate their students' use of AI to ensure they are actually learning the content rather than regurgitating what AI generates for them. Also, I do think that as AI becomes more integrated in the world, there will eventually need to be more of a focus on teaching about the ethical use of AI, especially in the workplace. I also think there will always be room for improvement in the course curriculum, as the landscape of software engineering is everchanging and it is important to keep students up-to-date.

## VIII. Conclusion
To conclude, I feel that AI should generally be used in a way that you are still learning and understanding concepts rather than just using whatever is generated without giving it thought. This should continue to be the case even as AI grows more prominent in software engineering education and undoubtedly becomes a useful tool for teaching students despite some challenges along the way. My recommendation for optimizing the integration of AI in future courses is to get people started early. In a more broader sense, the sooner you start trying to implement AI (or at least test it), the faster you can have people testing out and using the AI, and it will be faster to optimize its implementation across other students. On a smaller scale, I would say get students used to using the AI early on and set expectations and guidelines for however the professors want it to be used. That way, students will be prepared and ready to build their skills on day one.
